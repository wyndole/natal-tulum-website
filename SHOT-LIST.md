# Natal photo shoot plan — late November trip

Gear: Sony a6400 · 18-200 zoom · Rokinon 12mm (manual) · Godox TT350 + XProII-S · tripod

## Camera setup (do once)
- RAW + JPEG, ISO 100, fixed white balance per room (Daylight is fine; fix in RAW)
- Rokinon 12mm: Menu → Release w/o Lens → Enable. Turn on Focus Peaking.
  At f/8, focus at ~1 m (3 ft) → everything from ~0.5 m to infinity is sharp.
- Bracketing: Drive → Cont. Bracket, 3 frames, 2.0 EV (5 frames for rooms with bright windows)
  + Bracket Settings → Self-timer during bracket: 2 sec (no touching the camera)
- Turn on the level gauge (DISP). Keep the camera level — don't tilt up/down (walls lean).
- Shoot horizontal. The site uses landscape 4:3 tiles and a wide cover image.

## Staging checklist (before every room)
- All lights ON, curtains OPEN, ceiling fans OFF, TV off
- Hide cables, remotes, chargers, bags, cleaning supplies; remove tags from bed frames
- Beds made tight, pillows plumped, a throw; towels folded; toilet lids down
- Fresh flowers or a fruit bowl on the dining table/kitchen counter
- Close cabinet doors/closets (unless showing storage)

## How to use the gear
- Interiors: tripod at chest height (~1.2 m / 4 ft; a bit lower in bathrooms), 12mm, f/8, bracket.
- Flash for interiors: TT350 on the hot shoe, head turned up/back to bounce off the ceiling or wall
  behind you, TTL at −0.7. Take one bracket set without flash and one with — I can blend them.
  For bigger rooms: XProII on the camera, TT350 off-camera on a shelf/counter out of frame,
  bounced into a corner behind you. This fills shadows and stops windows blowing out.
- Warm bulbs + daylight = orange cast (the problem in the current bathroom shots). Bounce flash
  fixes most of it; also shoot one frame with the room lights OFF in bathrooms.
- Daytime outdoors with shade (terrace, patio): TT350 with HSS for fill so the shade isn't dark.
- Details: 18-200 at 50–135mm, f/5.6, handheld is fine.
- Polarizer (outdoors only): rotate while looking at the pool until reflections drop and the water turns
  turquoise. Take it OFF indoors (costs ~1.5 stops) and on the 12mm for skies (makes the sky blotchy).
  A budget polarizer can add a slight color cast or soften the image; shoot RAW and take one frame without it.
